Misplaced at Home? A Partial Observability Approach to Foster Care Placement Decisions

Abstract: When children are referred to Child Protective Services (CPS) caseworkers must decide whether to remove them from their homes or allow them to remain with their families. Although a large literature examines the consequences of foster care placement, much less is known about children who remain at home despite exhibiting characteristics similar to those of removed children. A central challenge is that both a child's underlying removal risk and whether placement decisions align with that risk are unobserved. This paper develops a partial observability framework to predict unobserved removal risk and foster care placement decisions using a nationally representative longitudinal sample of children referred to CPS. Further, I examine how latent removal risk and placement status affect subsequent educational outcomes.

Unaccompanied Migrant Youth and Child Labor Violations: Accounting for Nonclassical Measurement Error

Abstract: The number of unaccompanied minors apprehended at the U.S. border has surged in recent years, alongside a sharp rise in child labor violations under the Fair Labor Standards Act, many concentrated in industries like meatpacking, agriculture, and food processing. This paper asks whether the release of unaccompanied migrant children to sponsors contributes to rising child labor violations across U.S. counties. Combining Department of Labor investigation records from 2015 to 2023 with county level data on unaccompanied minors from the Office of Refugee Resettlement, we address a key measurement challenge: enforcement data likely capture only a fraction of true violations. Using a stochastic frontier framework that models the gap between detected and actual violations, we recover more credible estimates of both the scale of child labor violations and their relationship to youth migration flows.
